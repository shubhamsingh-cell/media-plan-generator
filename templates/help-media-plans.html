How to use the Media Plan Generator and Nova

A short guide for the people who build plans and send them to clients. This page describes what the tools do today; if the tool and this page ever disagree, trust the tool and tell us.

What it is for

Media plan generator

A five-step form (Client Info, Industry & Targeting, Channels, Extra Sections, Review & Export) that turns a client brief into an Excel workbook and a PowerPoint deck. Use it when you need a full plan to share.

Open the generator

Nova chat

A chat assistant for recruitment-marketing questions: average costs for a role, how two job boards compare, compliance requirements, how Joveo builds a media plan. Use it for quick answers and to talk through a plan.

Open Nova

Both are AI-assisted. Treat what they produce as a strong first draft that you review, not a finished answer. You are responsible for what you send to a client.

Enter a brief

The more specific the brief, the more specific the plan. These are the fields that matter most.

Budget

Type the amount the way you would say it: 150,000, 1.5M, 1.5 million, 500K or 5 lakh. A range such as 10k-15k is planned at its midpoint. The form shows you the figure it read, so check that line under the box.

Then choose what the amount covers: per campaign (total), per month, per quarter or per year. Weekly, daily and hourly budgets are not supported; enter a monthly amount instead.

Locations

Add each place on its own, as City, Country for anywhere outside the US, for example London, UK or Singapore. US locations work as Dallas, TX. Press Enter or type a comma after each one. Including the country name makes sure an international location is planned in the right country.

Roles, volume and timing

Add each target role (type it and press Enter), the number of hires, the campaign duration and, if it is not this month, the start month. The brief box and the optional uploads (PDF, DOCX or TXT, up to 7 MB in total) and call notes are used as context for the plan.

Excluding job boards

If the client does not want a board in the plan, add it under Exclude job boards (Channels step). Any spelling works: “Indeed”, “indeed.com” and “Indeed France” are treated as the same board. Excluded boards are removed from every sheet and slide, and the plan states what was excluded at the client’s request.

While you type

A live estimate (budget, applications, hires, cost per application) updates as you fill the form. It is a preview. Read the figures in the files you download.

What you get

  • Excel workbook. Starts with an Executive Summary, then Channels & Strategy, Market Intelligence and Sources & Confidence, followed by further sheets that depend on the plan (for example ROI projections, a forecast and international benchmarks). The last sheet is Review before sending.
  • PowerPoint deck. A strategy deck with an executive summary, channel strategy, budget allocation, competitive landscape, timeline and risks. The speaker notes on the first slide hold the same review checklist.
  • Quality check. If the automated check flags problems, a “Quality Check Found Issues” box appears before the download. Read it. Downloading anyway is your call, not a default.

Read the numbers

Estimates and assumptions

Projected hires and costs are estimates, not promises: they divide the media budget by an assumed cost per hire — Nova's own industry table (an all-in figure with no external source) or, where one exists, a cited local range — and the plan names which. When no client hire history was provided, say so when you present them and treat them as indicative. Where the plan shows a range instead of a single number (the projected hires, for example), quote the range. Where the plan states an assumption (for example, what it assumes per hire on the executive summary slide), read it before you quote the figure.

The † mark on a board

A job board shown with † has no confirmed place in the Joveo network for that market. Either Joveo’s supply repository lists it there without the status “In Joveo Network” (for example “VONQ Only” or “Via Redwood”), or the repository has no row for it in that market. No row means unknown, not unavailable, and some older supply notes call such a board confirmed while the repository does not list it; the workbook says so beside the board. The note reads “relationship unverified — confirm before quoting”. Do not promise that board to a client until supply confirms it.

Each local board in the workbook also carries its status with the date the data gives, for example “In Joveo Network, as of 2026-03-31”. A board that is “Not available through Joveo” is never shown in a plan. The boards on the deck are chosen by market share, not matched to the role; the line under them says so.

Data as of

The Executive Summary and the deck’s second slide state two separate supply dates: when the supply data (the full listing) is dated, and how far individual board availability has been updated since. A newer board date does not mean the full listing was refreshed. The Sources & Confidence sheet breaks the dates down by source family (board availability updates, supply data, benchmarks, salary data, seasonal patterns and so on). Each date is read from the data file itself. A source with no recorded date says date not recorded; nothing is estimated. The workbook and deck also carry the time they were generated and the app version.

Confidence

The Sources & Confidence sheet gives an overall data confidence grade, and shows how many live data sources returned data for this plan. Read the grade before you rely on the numbers.

Before you send

Open the Review before sending sheet (last tab of the workbook) or the speaker notes on slide 1 of the deck. The list is built from this plan, so it only mentions what applies. It can include:

  • Projected hires and costs are estimates from Nova's assumed industry cost-per-hire table (an all-in figure, not a published benchmark); media budget divided by that figure — no client hire history was provided. Treat them as indicative, not as commitments.
  • How many boards are marked †.
  • A budget reality check that flagged the budget.
  • Markets that have no local benchmarks.
  • The job boards the client asked you to exclude.
  • Always: check the brief details, confirm the channel mix with supply, check the data dates, and read everything yourself.

Nothing is sent for you. The files are yours to review, correct and send.

When to involve supply

Bring in the supply team before you commit anything to a client when:

  • The plan shows boards marked †.
  • A market is flagged as having no local benchmarks, or no board data. Figures there are indicative.
  • You are about to commit spend. Confirm the channel mix and the budget split first.
  • The budget reality check says the budget is under-funded.

Start over

  • Generator. The form keeps what you typed while the browser tab stays open. To begin a new brief, use Clear Form, next to the Generate button. It asks you to click twice, so one stray click does not wipe your inputs.
  • Nova. Choose New Chat to begin a new conversation. Earlier conversations are listed in the sidebar. Start a new chat when you switch clients, to keep each client’s conversation separate.

FAQ

Can I send the files straight to a client?

Only after you have reviewed them. Work through the Review before sending list, check the numbers you plan to quote, and correct anything you cannot stand behind.

Why does a board have a dagger next to it?

Its relationship with Joveo is not verified for that market. Confirm with supply before you quote it. See Read the numbers.

How current is the data?

Look at the “Data as of” line on the Executive Summary, or the per-source table on the Sources & Confidence sheet. If a source reads “date not recorded”, treat it with extra care.

A client does not want certain job boards. What now?

Add them under Exclude job boards before you generate. They are removed from the workbook and deck. If you notice one that slipped through, tell us rather than editing around it.

My budget was read differently than I expected.

Check the line under the budget box, which shows the figure the plan will use, and the period you picked (campaign total, month, quarter or year). A range is planned at its midpoint.

Where do I ask Nova about a plan I just made?

Use Ask Nova in the generator header. It opens Nova in a new tab with your brief or plan.
